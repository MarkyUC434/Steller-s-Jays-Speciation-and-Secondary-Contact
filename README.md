This is the associated GitHub Repository for the manuscript titled "Speciation, crest color evolution, and secondary contact in the Middle American Steller’s Jays (Cyanocitta stelleri)"

Secondary contact between differentiated lineages can produce complex patterns of genomic and phenotypic variation, particularly in montane regions. Steller’s Jays (Cyanocitta stelleri) range from Alaska to Nicaragua and exhibit striking geographic variation, but populations in Mexico and Central America remain poorly studied. We combined SNPs drawn from ultraconserved elements, phylogenetic analyses, mitochondrial divergence dating, and morphological data to investigate diversification across the species’ range, emphasizing the complex history of the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt (TMVB). Genomic analyses identified five geographic groups: Coastal, Rocky Mountain/Sierra Madre Occidental, Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt (TMVB), Southern Mexico, and Chiapas/Central America. Mitochondrial divergence estimates suggest a deep Pliocene split between Coastal populations and all others, followed by Pleistocene diversification across Middle America. Black-crested populations predominate in the north and blue-crested populations in the south, but the TMVB contains black-, blue-, and mixed-crested birds. TMVB populations form a distinct genomic cluster intermediate between northern and southern groups, but have variable and intermediate bill morphologies. These patterns are consistent with secondary contact of black- and blue-crested lineages, rather than a stepwise progression of diverged subspecies. The geographic transition resembles that between Spotted and Collared Towhees in the TMVB, suggesting that this mountain system may repeatedly structure contact among differentiated montane birds. Our results support recognizing three species-level lineages within the Steller’s Jay complex and illustrate how lineage divergence and secondary contact can lead to reticulate evolution in mountain ranges.
